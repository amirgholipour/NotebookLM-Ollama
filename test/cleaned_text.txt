Distillation Pipeline in LLM Era

The distillation pipeline is a structured process for transferring knowledge from a sophisticated teacher model to a less complex student model.

Knowledge Elicitation
The first stage involves the elicitation of relevant knowledge from the teacher model. This step is critical in identifying the key concepts and features that need to be transferred to the student model.

Distillation Algorithm
The second stage employs a distillation algorithm that quantifies the similarity between the teacher model's output and the desired output. This algorithm drives the learning process, optimizing the weights of the student model to minimize the difference between the two outputs.

Generated Knowledge
The third stage involves generating knowledge by combining the distilled student model with additional information or knowledge from other sources. This step is essential in filling gaps in the knowledge representation of the student model.

Learning Objectives
The final stage focuses on defining learning objectives for the student model, ensuring that it aligns with the desired knowledge outcomes. This stage is crucial in achieving the goals of the distillation pipeline.

A well-structured distillation pipeline enables the advanced capabilities of models like GPT-4 or Gemini to be leveraged in more accessible and efficient open-source counterparts.
Target Skill or Domain Steering Teacher LLM. The first stage involves directing the teacher LLM towards a specific target skill or domain through carefully crafted instructions or templates that guide its focus. This directs the LLM to respond with proficiency in a particular area such as healthcare or law, or skill such as reasoning or language understanding.

Seed Knowledge as Input. Once the target area is defined, seed knowledge is fed into the teacher LLM. This comprises a small dataset or specific data clues relevant to the skill or domain knowledge from the teacher LLM. The seed knowledge acts as a catalyst prompting the teacher LLM to generate more elaborate and detailed outputs based on this initial information.
thereby creating more comprehensive and in-depth knowledge examples.  In response to the seed knowledge and steering instructions, the teacher LLM generates knowledge examples. These examples are predominantly in the form of question-and-answer dialogues or narrative explanations, aligning with the natural language processing/understanding capabilities of the LLM. In certain specialized cases, the outputs may also include logits or hidden features, although this is less common due to the complexity and specific requirements of such data forms. The generated knowledge examples constitute the core of the distillation knowledge, encapsulating the advanced understanding and skills of the teacher LLM. This section explores advanced methods of distilling knowledge into student models using reinforcement learning (RL). This approach is especially relevant for leveraging feedback from the teacher to train student models. The RL-based distillation process typically involves two main stages:

Distilled Reward Model Training. 
The first stage
involves training a reward model rϕusing the feedback data D(fd) generated by teacher LLMs. Preference data, as one of the typical feedback, is employed to train the student reward model.
