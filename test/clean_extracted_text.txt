1 A Survey on Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models

Authors: Xiaohan Xu (The University of Hong Kong), Ming Li (University of Maryland), Chongyang Tao (Microsoft), Tao Shen (University of Technology Sydney), Reynold Cheng (The University of Hong Kong), Jinyang Li (The University of Hong Kong), Can Xu (Peking University), Dacheng Tao (The University of Sydney), Tianyi Zhou (University of Maryland)

Abstract: In the era of Large Language Models (LLMs), Knowledge Distillation (KD) serves as a crucial methodology for transferring advanced capabilities from leading proprietary LLMs, such as GPT-4, to their open-source counterparts like LLaMA and Mistral. Furthermore, KD plays a significant role in both compressing these models and facilitating their self-improvement by employing themselves as teachers. This paper provides a comprehensive survey of KD's role within the realm of LLMs, emphasizing its critical function in imparting advanced capabilities.
Advanced knowledge distillation (KD) techniques and their application in model compression and self-improvement are explored in this survey. The survey is organized around three main aspects: algorithms, skills, and verticalization. It delves into various KD mechanisms, the enhancement of specific cognitive abilities, and their practical applications across different domains.

A significant focus is on the relationship between data augmentation (DA) and KD. The survey demonstrates how DA serves as a potent strategy within the KD framework to enhance large language models' (LLMs) performance. By utilizing DA to create context-rich, skill-specific training data, KD surpasses conventional limitations, allowing open-source models to mimic the contextual proficiency, ethical alignment, and profound semantic understanding typically associated with proprietary models.

This research aims to offer a valuable resource for scholars and professionals, providing a thorough review of existing methodologies in knowledge distillation.

Large Language Models (LLMs) have unlocked new possibilities, from generating human-like text to offering advanced problem-solving abilities. Their core significance lies in emergent abilities, where models exhibit capabilities beyond their explicit training objectives, excelling at understanding and generation tasks. These models drive applications from creative content creation to complex problem-solving.

The term 'proprietary' encompasses both versatile, closed-source LLMs like GPT-4 and large, open-source LLMs such as LLaMA-2-70B, which store extensive knowledge through a vast number of parameters. The potential of these models extends far beyond current applications, poised to revolutionize industries, enhance human creativity, and more.

Despite the impressive capabilities of proprietary large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 and Gemini, they have notable drawbacks, especially when compared to open-source alternatives. A major issue is their limited accessibility and higher cost (OpenAI et al., 2023). These proprietary models often involve substantial usage fees and restricted access, making them less accessible for individuals and smaller organizations.

Regarding data privacy and security (Wu et al., 2023a), using these proprietary LLMs typically requires sending sensitive data to external servers, raising concerns about data privacy and security. This is particularly concerning for users dealing with confidential information. Furthermore, the general-purpose design of proprietary LLMs, while powerful, may not always align with the specific needs of niche applications. The limitations in accessibility, cost, and adaptability thus present challenges.
Presenting significant challenges in fully utilizing proprietary Language Learning Models (LLMs). Unlike proprietary LLMs, open-source alternatives such as LLaMA (Touvron et al., 2023) and Mistral (Jiang et al., 2023a) offer distinct advantages. Key among these is their accessibility and adaptability. Unencumbered by licensing fees or stringent usage rules, these models are more accessible to a wider user base, from solo researchers to smaller entities. This openness cultivates a collaborative and inclusive AI research community, promoting innovation and diverse applications. Furthermore, the customizable nature of open-source LLMs enables more tailored solutions, addressing specific requirements that broad, large-scale models might overlook. Nevertheless, open-source LLMs also have limitations, mainly due to their comparatively smaller scale.
