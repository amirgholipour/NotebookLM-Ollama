Expansion methods can significantly boost NLP task dataset performance by adding similar samples. For example, AugGPT (Dai et al., 2023a) employs a teacher LLM to generate multiple conceptually similar, yet semantically varied, sentences from training samples to improve classification performance. Similarly, TDG (He et al., 2023b) proposes the Targeted Data Generation (TDG) framework.
The expansion method utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) for in-context learning to identify complex subgroups within data and create new samples for these subgroups. It aims to generate more diverse and extensive datasets, including both inputs and outputs. However, the quality and variety of the generated data are contingent on the teacher LLMs and initial seed demonstrations. This reliance can introduce bias from LLMs (Yu et al., 2023a; Wei et al., 2023) and homogeneity issues, causing generations to be similar, thereby limiting the desired diversity (Ding et al., 2023b). Additionally, the expansion process may unintentionally exacerbate biases present in the seed data.

In response to these challenges, the Data Curation approach has emerged. This method focuses on achieving high-quality and scalable data generation from LLMs through careful curation.
Data Curation tackles the limitations of Labeling and Expansion methods by curating high-quality or large-scale data using extensive meta-information as seed knowledge.
